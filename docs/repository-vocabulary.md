# Forma Repository Vocabulary

The words Forma uses for its own concepts, used the same way in code, tokens, documents and the Showcase. A page is a canvas with backdrops laid on it, the way VS Code's sections stand on its workbench. A backdrop is told apart from the backdrop beside it by tone or by the seam between them, never by a line drawn across it.

- A **backdrop** is a region of the page, `FormaBackdrop`.
- A **variant** is the look a backdrop takes, `BackdropVariant`, and every backdrop has exactly one: **Flat** lies in its parent and is told apart by tone alone; **Raised** stands off the canvas as a card with a corner and a small shadow; **Inset** is set back a tone, as a header strip or a toolbar band; **Canvas** is the backdrop other backdrops are laid on.
- The **canvas** is a backdrop in the Canvas variant. It is one variant among four, not a different kind of thing.
- A **seam** is the one spacing step of the parent, `--space-1`, showing between two backdrops.
- A **stack** is `FormaStackLayout`: backdrops along one axis, a seam apart, vertical or horizontal.
- A **scrim** is the translucent dark layer a modal stands on, dimming and blocking what lies beneath it: `--scrim-color`, the window's `forma-window-scrim`, and what a native dialog draws as its `::backdrop`. The word is the theater's, for the gauze that dims the scene behind it, and Material Design's for this layer; it is the library's only word for it, so that "backdrop" means the surface alone.
- A **frame** is `FormaFrame`: a raised backdrop with a box of its own. In a layout it takes the room it is given; placed, it stands in the viewport, moved by the drag region its host designates, resized at its edges and maximized to its bounds. Presented by a parent that says so, it drives that parent's box instead. It can be hidden or disabled, and it knows no workspace.
- A **chrome** is `FormaChrome`: a frame whose drag region is a title bar carrying the icon, the title and the host's commands, with an optional header band, the body and an optional footer band inside the frame.
- A **window** is `FormaWindow`: a chrome floated into the top layer and a member of the workspace, whose frame drives the window's box. It is activated, stacked, minimized, locked and closed; a frame and a chrome are none of those. A window never embeds. A dialog is a modal window.
- A **workspace** is the set of windows one `WindowManager` coordinates, the way a desktop holds the windows on it: which is current, their stacking order, which window opened which, what a minimize or a close takes along, and what the rest area lists. A circuit has one by default; a host that cascades another manager makes a nested one.
- A **component** is a thing Forma ships, and an **element** is raw HTML; one word per concept. "Control" is Web Forms vocabulary from the legacy application; it survives for a native form control, what `Controls.js` and the focus ring address, in `aria-controls`, and in descriptions of that application's own markup.
- A component's **root** is the element the host addresses: for most components the outermost element they render, for an input the control inside its field, for a composite the control the host names and styles. The root carries the host's `Id`, `Class`, `Style` and attribute bag, and is `RootElement`.
- A **runtime id**, `RuntimeId`, is the identity a component instance carries for its lifetime: a `Guid` written on its root as `data-rid`, the stem of the ids it generates for its elements, the name a log line and a workspace know it by. It is never the DOM `id`, which belongs to the consumer.
- A **component context**, `IComponentContext`, is what a component is given: its runtime id, its strings, its log and its JS environment, built once by the base and read through `Context`, with `L` as its one forward. A thing every component needs is added to the context, never threaded through the bases or the wrappers one by one. `Context` is reserved for it: anything else context-like a component holds is named for its subject alone, `Composer` for the composer parts' cascaded state, `JS` for the environment.
- A **JS environment**, `IJSEnvironment`, is the runtime a component's interop wrapper calls, carrying the component's log and runtime id: an `IJSRuntime` that also says whose calls these are, so a wrapper logs under its owner and names the instance. A component reaches its own as `Context.JS`; a service that owns a wrapper builds one from the runtime and logger it takes.
- A **data view** is `FormaDataView`, the library's collection component: an engine that manages a set of items and the state around them, its source, selection, sort, page, address, grouping and states, and hands what is on screen to a **view**, built in or the host's own, that presents it. It can be a simple list of items or a grid, cards or a tree. The **active view** is the one showing.
- A **grid** is rows with aligned columns and headers, `FormaDataGrid`; a **list** is items arranged down the height, `FormaDataList`; **cards** are items with an image, a title and other information, `FormaDataCards`; a **tree** is items as nodes under their parents, `FormaDataTree`. These name the components; a consumer names the modes it composes them in for its own readers, as the explorer calls its cards view thumbnails.
- A **thumbnail** is an item's depiction, the picture a card or a list row draws. A surface showing thumbnails composes the cards view at its compact density, the image and its name alone; there is no fourth view.
- A **slice** is the run of positions a provider is asked for, given by its start and its count, which the provider answers with those items; the answer is a page, `DataViewPage` or `CatalogPage`, and a slice shorter than asked for ends a set the provider cannot count.
- A **rendered range** is the run of items a virtualized list draws around the viewport: the items the viewport shows and the overscan beyond them. An item its owner pins outside it is drawn as well, as a further, disjoint run.
