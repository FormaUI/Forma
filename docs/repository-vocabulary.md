# Forma Repository Vocabulary

The words Forma uses for its own concepts, used the same way in code, tokens, documents and the Showcase. A page is a canvas with backdrops laid on it, the way VS Code's sections stand on its workbench. A backdrop is told apart from the backdrop beside it by tone or by the seam between them.

- A **backdrop** is a region of the page, `FormaBackdrop`.
- A **variant** is the look a backdrop takes, `BackdropVariant`, and every backdrop has exactly one: **Flat** lies in its parent and is told apart by tone alone; **Raised** stands off the canvas as a card with a corner and a small shadow; **Inset** is set back a tone, as a header strip or a toolbar band; **Canvas** is the backdrop other backdrops are laid on.
- The **canvas** is a backdrop in the Canvas variant, one variant among the four.
- A **seam** is the one spacing step of the parent, `--space-1`, showing between two backdrops.
- A **stack** is `FormaStackLayout`: backdrops along one axis, a seam apart, vertical or horizontal.
- A **scrim** is the translucent dark layer a modal stands on, dimming and blocking what lies beneath it: `--scrim-color`, the window's `forma-window-scrim`, and what a native dialog draws as its `::backdrop`. It is the library's one word for that layer, so "backdrop" means the surface alone.
- A **frame** is `FormaFrame`: a raised backdrop with a box of its own. In a layout it takes the room it is given; placed, it stands in the viewport, moved by the drag region its host designates, resized at its edges and maximized to its bounds. Presented by a parent that says so, it drives that parent's box instead. It can be hidden or disabled, and it knows no workspace.
- A **chrome** is `FormaChrome`: a frame whose drag region is a title bar carrying the icon, the title and the host's commands, with an optional header band, the body and an optional footer band inside the frame.
- A **window** is `FormaWindow`: a chrome floated into the top layer and a member of the workspace, whose frame drives the window's box. It is activated, stacked, minimized, locked and closed, and it always floats. A modal child blocks its parent's subtree, and a modal root the workspace.
- A **dialog** is `FormaDialog`: the browser's native modal `<dialog>`, prompting for a decision in the top layer, beside the workspace. A modal member of the workspace is a window with `Modal` set.
- A **workspace** is the set of windows one `WindowManager` coordinates, the way a desktop holds the windows on it: which is current, their stacking order, which window opened which, what a minimize or a close takes along, and what the rest area lists. A user scope has one by default; a host that cascades another manager makes a nested one.
- The **rest area** is `FormaRestArea`: one persistent handle per window in the workspace, the way back to any window whatever its state.
- A **user scope** is what one user's interactive components share: a circuit under Interactive Server, a WebAssembly runtime in the browser, a web view in a Blazor Hybrid application. A scoped service has one instance per user scope, so the input router and the default workspace are each one per user scope, as the culture state is everywhere but in a Blazor Hybrid application, which holds one for all its web views.
- A **web view** is a `BlazorWebView` in one of a Blazor Hybrid application's native windows, rendering the components to its own document with a scope of its own; every web view of an application shares its services and its one culture. A **native window** is the operating system's window a WPF, Windows Forms or MAUI application opens, never Forma's window.
- An **island** is an interactive region of a page whose router renders statically; a page can hold islands of Interactive Server beside islands of WebAssembly, and the islands of one render mode share its user scope.
- A **render-mode half** is the assembly that carries what one render mode needs and the registrar that adds it: `Forma.Blazor.Server` with `AddFormaUIServer` for circuits, `Forma.Blazor.WebAssembly` with `AddFormaUIWebAssembly` for the browser, `Forma.Blazor.WebView` with `AddFormaUIWebView` for an application's web views.
- The **culture state**, `ICultureState`, is the pair of cultures a user scope renders in: the culture that formats dates, numbers and collation, and the UI culture that chooses strings and direction. A change of it is requested through the **switch**, `ICultureSwitch`.
- A **locale pack** is an assembly of locale files alone, carrying the strings of the assemblies it localizes in one language.
- The **input router**, `InputRouter`, is the one place that decides which component a key, a press, a click, a wheel turn or a drop goes to, and an **input scope** is an interaction context registered with it on an element, owning the input that originates within it, as a popup, a dialog or a grid does.
- A **component** is a thing Forma ships, and an **element** is raw HTML; one word per concept. "Control" names a native form control, what `Controls.js` and the focus ring address, and appears in `aria-controls` and in descriptions of the legacy application's markup.
- A component's **root** is the element the host addresses: for most components the outermost element they render, for an input the control inside its field, for a composite the control the host names and styles. The root carries the host's `Id`, `Class`, `Style` and attribute bag, and is `RootElement`.
- A **runtime id**, `RuntimeId`, is the identity a component instance carries for its lifetime: a `Guid` written on its root as `data-rid`, the stem of the ids it generates for its elements, the name a log line and a workspace know it by. The DOM `id` stays the consumer's.
- A **component context**, `IComponentContext`, is what a component is given: its runtime id, its strings, its log, its JS environment, and the DOM watcher and engine its browser work goes through, built once by the base and read through `Context`, with `L` as its one forward. `Context` is reserved for it: anything else context-like a component holds is named for its subject alone, `Composer` for the composer parts' cascaded state, `JS` for the environment.
- A **JS environment**, `IJSEnvironment`, is the runtime a component's interop wrapper calls, carrying the component's log and runtime id: an `IJSRuntime` that also says whose calls these are, so a wrapper logs under its owner and names the instance. A component reaches its own as `Context.JS`; a service that owns a wrapper builds one from the runtime and logger it takes.
- A **DOM watcher**, `DomWatcher`, is what an owner observes its elements through, and a **DOM engine**, `DomEngine`, what it acts on them through, borrowing its watcher. An **observation**, `DomObservation`, is state the watcher keeps measuring and hands to its owner; an **interaction**, `DomInteraction`, is what the engine keeps running on an element, such as a wheel route or step buttons. Each is kept in step with what its owner last asked for through `SyncAsync`. A component reaches both as `Context.DomWatcher` and `Context.DomEngine`.
- A **data view** is `FormaDataView`, the library's collection component: an engine that manages a set of items and the state around them, its source, selection, sort, page, address, grouping and states, and hands what is on screen to a **view**, built in or the host's own, that presents it. It can be a simple list of items or a grid, cards or a tree. The **active view** is the one showing.
- A **grid** is rows with aligned columns and headers, `FormaDataGrid`; a **list** is items arranged down the height, `FormaDataList`; **cards** are items with an image, a title and other information, `FormaDataCards`; a **tree** is items as nodes under their parents, `FormaDataTree`. These name the components; a consumer names the modes it composes them in for its own readers, as a file manager calls its cards view thumbnails.
- A **thumbnail** is an item's depiction, the picture a card or a list row draws. A surface showing thumbnails composes the cards view at its compact density, the image and its name alone.
- A **slice** is the run of positions a provider is asked for, given by its start and its count, which the provider answers with those items; the answer is a page, `DataViewPage` or `CatalogPage`, and a slice shorter than asked for ends a set the provider cannot count.
- A **rendered range** is the run of items a virtualized list draws around the viewport: the items the viewport shows and the overscan beyond them. An item its owner pins outside it is drawn as well, as a further, disjoint run.
