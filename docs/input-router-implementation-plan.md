# Forma Input Router Implementation Plan

The plan for bringing every input a component acts on under the router that [the input router design](input-router-design.md) states, and the record of what has landed. Section names refer to the design.

## Status

The router arbitrates the keys, presses, wheel, drags and drops of most behaviors: the drag gesture, the drop zone, focus, reorder, the selection keys, the selection follower and the wheel register with it. Two presses are still acted on outside it, each read through a handler of the component's own: the splitter's, which begins a resize, and the window's, which brings the window to the front; see the open decisions.

Phase 1 has landed: the guard is gone, Enter on a data view's marker is the selection behavior's activation route, and the pager's jump is an Enter route on its box. The selection behavior now attaches under checked selection as well as item selection, declaring only the activation there, since under checked selection the boxes select and a press or an arrow has nothing to do; the item comes from the marker the key was pressed on, which the router does not pass to a handler in .NET, so activation is told to the data view by the script rather than routed to a handler of its own.

Phase 2 has landed: the search box runs its search on an Enter route on its field, the select closes its list on a Tab route that keeps the key's default so the focus still moves on, the tree's type-ahead is a route on a typing binding, a binding of the keyboard family that names no key and matches any printable character other than a space, and no library component reads a key through a handler of its own. The popover no longer forwards keys to its content, since its one consumer, the select, closes on Tab through a route; content answers its keys through the routes it declares. The pager's keys needed nothing beyond phase 1's jump. The pan gesture needed nothing either: it composes the drag gesture, whose registration is already active for as long as a drag lasts, so Escape during a pan already cancels it through the router.

Phase 3 has landed: clicks are a binding family of their own, a click on a native control is the control's, the click a claimed press ends in is swallowed, a click raised from the keyboard never is, and a click whose press stood in a scope since gone belongs to nothing. The drag gesture claims the press it turns into a drag, so a drag never ends in a click on what lay under it, and the window a swallowed click has runs from the release, since a drag lasts as long as the pointer is held. The selection behavior answers every press on a data view's items: where the view selects, a press selects, a shift-press stops the browser's own text selection at press time, and a double press activates; elsewhere a press activates, so the behavior attaches to every data view, and an item carries its token itself, since an item with no selection has no marker to carry one. The part of an item holding its own controls, a box's cell, a list's or a card's lead, a card's actions, a tree node's disclosure, is marked so a press there activates nothing, as the handlers that stopped the press there did before. The tree's sidebar chooses a node on a click route beside Enter, and its disclosure button fills its slot, so a press anywhere in the slot is the button's. Two things landed differently from the sketch: a double press is a binding of the click family naming the double click rather than a count, and a row being edited carries no token, so no press on it activates the row.

## Phases

1. **Enter.** ✅ The guard that stops Enter submitting the form around a control leaves the library, its script, wrapper, tests and inventory row with it. Enter becomes a route where a component acts on it: the selection behavior's activation of the item whose marker holds the focus, which replaces the views' own Enter handlers on their markers, and the pager's jump. The scripting guide gains the rule that input a component acts on is a route or an active registration on the router. Gate: Enter on a marker and in the pager's jump input act without submitting a surrounding form, and nothing in the library listens for Enter outside the router.
2. **The remaining keys.** ✅ The popover's, the search box's and the pager's keys become routes on their scopes, the tree's type-ahead a route matching any printable character, and the pan gesture an active registration while it pans, as the drag gesture is. Gate: no library component reads a key through its own handler, and Escape during a pan resolves as it does during a drag.
3. **Click selection through the router.** ✅ Click becomes a binding family of its own beside the keyboard, pointer, wheel and drop families, dispatched on the click event rather than as shorthand for a pointer's release, so a click raised from the keyboard resolves like any other with no preceding press required. A press resolves its scope and lets a gesture begin without committing a selection; an active registration that claims the gesture, a drag or a pan, marks the click that follows it suppressed, tied to its pointer and the element pressed, and a click raised from the keyboard is never suppressed; the click then dispatches its route once suppression has been applied. A scope that goes away or turns inactive drops the click pending on it rather than letting it fall to the scope beneath. The selection behavior's click becomes a click route and its shift-press, which stops the browser selecting text, a pointer route at press time as it is today; the views' own presses on an item, the press that activates it and the double press that activates it under a selection, become click routes too. Gate: a short drag inside a row selects nothing, a click after it selects normally, a keyboard click is never swallowed, and Enter and a synthesized click never run one action twice.

## Open decisions

- **The splitter's press.** The splitter begins a resize from a press it reads itself, reading the pointer and its position. A pointer route on the splitter would answer it the same way, since the router hands a .NET owner the pointer's identity and position with the action; it waits only on being agreed as a phase of its own.
- **The window's press.** The window comes to the front on any press inside it, a press an inner scope may also own: a splitter or a drag inside the window routes its press, and a route on the window would then never hear it. Bringing a window forward observes a press rather than owning it, so it either stays outside the router as the one observer the rule allows, stated so in the design, or the router grows a way for a scope to hear presses within it that another scope owns.
